t07 — The USB image: what writing it does, proven against a file

Writing a USB stick means writing this raw image over the
whole device (see the project's docs; the guide's USB
chapter is documented-only because the lab has no USB device
to give). The write itself can be rehearsed against an
ordinary file, which proves the image copies byte-for-byte:

$ dd if=downloads/netboot.xyz.img of=usb-copy.img bs=1M status=none
$ sha256sum downloads/netboot.xyz.img usb-copy.img
bcb1f30942a2d5f2a81326fd6c585a0687578a49c33d3713dff951c777c27885  downloads/netboot.xyz.img
bcb1f30942a2d5f2a81326fd6c585a0687578a49c33d3713dff951c777c27885  usb-copy.img

(usb-copy.img deleted after the check.)

Facts about the image, from t01: exactly 2 MiB
(2,097,152 bytes = 4096 sectors of 512), a DOS/MBR disk
with one active FAT partition. It is not an installer for
anything — it is the iPXE bootloader on a stick: boot it,
and it does DHCP and chains to the menu like any other
netboot.xyz bootloader. The 2 MiB size is the whole point:
any cast-off USB key, however small and old, can hold it.
